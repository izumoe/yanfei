Core waveform review

The review used actual fixed-point engine renders and the running browser emulator, not the earlier idle screenshots. Diagnostic captures, plots and measurement scripts are in the local ignored build/waveform-review/ directory.

Core3 appends three vintage-inspired banks while retaining the original eight wave tables exactly. The extension's generator checks 20 distinct waves and all 80 harmonic mips; standalone renderer tests cover all five banks, four corners, scan motion, low/high notes and warp extremes. The core3 full-mix run has 106 cases and zero PCM rail hits. Core2's detailed measurements below remain a record of the base DSP; see CODE_REVIEW.md for the extension checks.

Verified behavior

  • Fifty dry single-voice captures cover all four pulse duties, the NES staircase, all sixteen DMG wave RAM shapes, short/long noise, every PSG envelope shape, chip decays and WAVE at low through very high notes.
  • Basic A4 pulse, triangle and sine outputs measure 440 Hz. NES/DMG pulse duty settings yield the expected 12.5/25/50/75% shapes; the triangle uses a 32-position staircase and DMG preserves its 32-position, 4-bit wave character.
  • The eleven engine UIs were inspected while sounding. FM6 EDIT intentionally displays its operator diagram, so HOME is used to view its output waveform.
  • All 33 factory presets were exercised with one-note and eight-note input through the actual mix, FX, master and audio simulation, 66 independent processes in total. Existing voice caps and mono modes still apply. All outputs were finite; no samples reached the PCM rails. Largest observed peak was approximately 0.391 full scale. This is not a claim of zero distortion: the existing soft saturation and limiter intentionally affect the output.

Corrections made

The first NES/DMG decay step previously ignored the selected frame-clock divider: it happened after one clock frame, even for a slow decay. Initialization now loads the divider as well as resetting the volume. For D7 the first step waits about 33.3 ms on NES and 125 ms on DMG, matching the following steps. Regression tests check both the full initial plateau and the first decrement.

PSG's reverse triangle envelope previously missed one level at its peak. It is now the exact 15-minus-triangle complement over the sixteen envelope positions. A regression test checks this relation for the complete cycle.

Interpreting the scope and remaining limits

The on-device scope shows the left channel of the output after effects and MASTER in OUT mode (MIX is an alternative). It auto-scales amplitude, displays every other audio sample and triggers on a rising zero crossing. Therefore its waveform can differ from the dry oscillator, equal visual heights do not imply equal loudness, and very high-frequency traces can alias in the display itself. Spectral checks use the full-rate samples instead.

Audio-rate PSG envelopes can introduce DC into the dry modulated waveform; the existing output DC blocker removes it before the final saturation stage. Asymmetric waves can acquire a small mean offset again through that nonlinear stage: the NES pulse preset measured roughly -0.0038 full scale on the left, and the upstream LOFI pulse preset similarly measured -0.0035. This is existing master-stage behavior, not evidence of an incorrect new pulse duty. No extra filter or per-voice state was added merely to compensate for it in the engines.

WAVE now crossfades harmonic limits before their bandwidth reaches Nyquist and reduces effective warp with increasing pitch. In the same 7,040 Hz test, measured off-harmonic energy is approximately -87 dB at WARP 0 and -87 dB at maximum WARP, compared with -39 dB at maximum warp in the first implementation. This is a result for this waveform and pitch, not a global aliasing bound. A regression checks folded harmonics 4..12 below -60 dB in this case. High-pitch warp intentionally becomes milder; there is no oversampling buffer or additional voice storage.

Chip pitch recalculation now retains the host's fine-tuning, pitch-bend residual and unison detune. NES, DMG, PSG and WAVE retain their filter state on sounding retriggers while restarting the chip envelopes. Integration and standalone tests exercise both behaviors.

Core integration tests, standalone chip tests, sanitizer checks, target loop-cost budgets and the native-versus-WASM audio comparison are rerun for these corrections. Real FM-1 timing and analogue outputs still require hardware; these captures also do not replace subjective listening.
